World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026

Russia and Ukraine agreed a large prisoner exchange as US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended, the Guardian reported, citing Reuters. President Zelenskyy said 1,000 or possibly 1,500 per side were under discussion; no peace deal was announced. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff were due in Moscow to see Putin.

The Justice Department charged three Iranian-backed operatives over nearly 20 plots in Europe and the US, including one at RAF Fairford. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to a military base after threats attributed to Iran, the New York Times reported, and 13 aircraft left RAF Mildenhall as it went to its second-highest alert level, sources told the Wall Street Journal. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said tankers caught fire after explosions in the Strait of Hormuz.

In Washington, the House voted 211-204 to shelve impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and Republicans elected Jim Jordan minority leader from January 2027.

British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia, and NATO jets scrambled after drones entered Baltic airspace. The FBI put Shahram Poursafi, accused of plotting to kill former official John Bolton, on its Most Wanted list. Nvidia's board hired a former prosecutor to examine chief Jensen Huang's ties to an alleged Chinese spy.

At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres told 102 leaders the climate crisis was 'outrunning us' and urged a fossil-fuel tax; the US stayed away for a second year. In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot struggled to form a government after a deadlocked election cost Benjamin Netanyahu his majority.

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% forecast, lifting December rate-rise expectations. Anthropic filed for a Nasdaq listing that could value it at $1.4 trillion. Russia's ambassador to the US, Alexander Darchiev, died suddenly at 66. Spain held its first general strike in 14 years over housing costs.
